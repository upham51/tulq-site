The requirement

After-hours coverage is a compliance line item, not a nice-to-have.

HRSA Health Center Program Compliance Manual · Chapter 7

Health centers and the Rural Health Clinics operating alongside them must demonstrate after-hours coverage. HRSA's own compliance manual states the health center "determines the means by which after-hours coverage is provided... Examples include... 'nurse call' lines" — a direct, citable federal endorsement of exactly this service.

42 CFR 485, Subpart F

Critical Access Hospitals carry a separate 24-hour emergency-coverage Condition of Participation: staff on-site or on-call, available on-site within 30 minutes (up to 60 in frontier areas). A small hospital without a physician in the building overnight still has to answer that requirement.

And for practices billing Advanced Primary Care Management, the 2025 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule created codes G0556 ($15.20), G0557 ($48.84), and G0558 ($107.07 per patient/month) — with 2026 rates rising roughly 10%. All three require 24/7 access to care as one of 13 service elements, which turns "nice to have" into a line item tied directly to reimbursement.

Questions

What clinic administrators ask us first.

Does a Rural Health Clinic need 24/7 nurse coverage to meet CMS requirements?

RHCs and the health centers they operate alongside must demonstrate after-hours coverage arrangements under HRSA Chapter 7, which explicitly names nurse call lines as an acceptable means of coverage. Critical Access Hospitals carry a separate 24-hour emergency-coverage Condition of Participation under 42 CFR 485, Subpart F.

What is the APCM 24/7 access requirement?

Advanced Primary Care Management (APCM) billing codes G0556-G0558, created in the 2025 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule, require practices to meet 13 service elements to bill Medicare — one of which is 24/7 access to care, commonly satisfied with a nurse triage line.
